Summary

Everyone Buys Leads. Few Turn Them Into Patients.

We looked at the practice's four closest competitors. We checked their ads, prices, websites, chat tools and opening hours. Then we rang each one as a new patient.

  • One competitor sets the standard. It runs ads on Facebook, Instagram and Google, tracks every call, offers a free chat with a treatment coordinator and holds open days with £500 off.
  • One competitor copies the client's price. It launched implant ads "from £2,500" the week we checked. That is the client's own published price.
  • Everyone answers. Few convert. All four picked up straight away. One let our caller go without a question. One booked her without asking what she wanted.
  • The client starts from a strong place. It has the most Google reviews of the five practices (about 700 at 4.9 stars). What it lacks is the system behind the ads.

The competitors

What Each One Does, and Where It Is Weak

Competitor A: city-centre private practice

  • 7 Facebook and Instagram ads and about 50 Google ads live.
  • Free consultation with a treatment coordinator, by phone, video or in person.
  • Open day with £500 off smile makeovers. Patients pay a £20 deposit to hold a slot.
  • Finance calculator on the website: 0% up to 12 months.
  • Follows website visitors with ads on Facebook, TikTok and other sites.
Weak spot: it costs a lot to run, and it relies on events and volume. A local practice can match its reply speed for far less money.

Competitor B: NHS and private practice

  • 4 Facebook and Instagram ads and 17 Google ads live, including Google Maps ads.
  • A chatbot opens on every page and collects the visitor's details.
  • Free consultation offer. No prices on the website.
Weak spot: the chatbot only takes details. Someone still has to call back, and the phones close for lunch and at 5.15pm. Whoever calls the patient first wins.

Competitor C: cosmetic group with several sites

  • About 49 live ads across the group, led by price: "8 veneers for £5,000" and "bonding from £250 a tooth".
  • Denture ads send patients straight to an online booking page.
  • Live chat on the website. No Google ads for a year.
Weak spot: cheap offers bring in bargain hunters. This site has about 50 Google reviews. The client has about 700.

Competitor D: boutique implant clinic

  • 5 new implant ads: "from £2,500", free consultation, £30 deposit.
  • An outside agency runs the ads and collects the forms.
  • Its own website still shows a dummy phone number and placeholder text on one page.
Weak spot: leads arrive as forms that reception must chase in office hours. Same price as the client, so the practice that calls back first and explains best wins.

What a good first call looks like

  1. Answer fastWithin a few rings, evenings and weekends too.
  2. Build rapportUse the patient's name. Show interest.
  3. Ask questionsWhat treatment, what bothers them, budget, timing.
  4. Explain the next stepConsultation, fee or deposit, and finance in plain numbers.
  5. Book on the callOffer two times and confirm one.
  6. Follow upText to confirm, reminders, and a call back if they did not book.

The practice's gaps

Where Our Client Was Losing Patients

Strengths

  • Most Google reviews of the five practices.
  • Prices published for most treatments.
  • Implant price matches the cheapest in the area.
  • Ads already running, with tracking in place.

Gaps

  • No Google ads. Two rivals pay to appear when locals search "dental implants".
  • No instant reply. Two rivals greet every visitor with chat. The client has a form.
  • No online booking. Three rivals let patients book themselves.
  • Vague finance. "Flexible payments" against a 0% calculator next door.
  • Price mismatch. Aligners £3,500 on the site, £3,750 in the ad.
  • No events. A rival fills a Saturday with an open day.

What we would do

Five Actions for the Next 30 Days

  1. 1. Reply to every enquiry within 5 minutes, 7 days a week. Our trained treatment coordinators call each lead, evenings and weekends included, and book the consultation.
  2. 2. Start a small Google campaign on local implant and aligner searches.
  3. 3. Show monthly finance figures on implants and aligners, and match site and ad prices.
  4. 4. Add a smile preview to the ads. Patients upload a photo and see their new smile before they book.
  5. 5. Run an evening consultation event as a local answer to the rival's open day.
